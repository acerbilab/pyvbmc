> ### The hyperprior is renormalized to the bounds, and a fixed hyperparameter gets a prior

## Anything I found beyond the two reports (mine)

1. **`sp.linalg.eig` breaks `U D Uᵀ = Σ` in `__robust_cholesky` independently of the sign flip** (G1-3b). The comparison report notes the `eig`/`eigh` substitution as an accuracy remark; it is more than that. With a repeated positive eigenvalue above the tolerance, the general solver need not return an orthogonal basis of that eigenspace, and then the reconstruction fails before any flip: `0.416` against `4.44e-16` with `eigh`, on `Q diag(1,1,1,0,0) Qᵀ`. A correct per-column sign flip alone would leave this in place, so the two fixes belong in one change.
2. **MATLAB's rank-one `L` and `sW` extension is wrong for heteroskedastic noise**, by `0.116` relative in the represented matrix and with an `sW` entry of `4.0824829` where `3.8033925` is needed. New MATLAB-side defect, tabled above; latent in VBMC because `gplite_post.m:76-79` refuses the path when the new point carries an `s2`.
3. **`update` on a GP with no hyperparameters produces NaN posteriors silently** rather than raising (G1-29), because `sp.linalg.cholesky(..., check_finite=False)` returns a NaN factor for a NaN matrix on this LAPACK build. This refutes internal M-M's stated consequence and is the more serious behavior.
4. **`VBMC.load(new_options=)` skips a validity check, not only an effect** (G1-X): `gp_mean_fun="nonsense"` and `integer_vars=[True, False]` are both accepted where construction raises or acts. That moves the row from "an option takes no coherent effect" to "an option bypasses its own validation".
5. **`nll` is searched beyond `Nopts` in the branch VBMC itself drives** (the `LogP`/`Ns > 0` branch of `gplite_train.m:180`), not only in the `Ninit == 0` branch comparison M12 names.

## Closing note on the three rows I left out

I came across two things bearing on the rows the orchestrator verifies itself.

**On comparison M6 (the variance estimate floored at zero) — a much sharper demonstration than the report has.** When `floor(burn/2) == 1`, that is for `burn ∈ {2, 3}`, gpyreg's window `burn/2 <= i < burn` accumulates exactly **one** iteration, so `xx_sq_sum/burn_stored − (xx_sum/burn_stored)²` is **exactly zero** and `np.maximum(..., 0)` gives every coordinate a width of exactly `0`. The chain then cannot move again: `G1_10_test_claims.py` (c) shows `widths = [0. 0.]` after burn-in and **1 distinct recorded sample out of 6** for `burn = 2` and `burn = 3`, against 6 of 6 and sensible widths for `burn ∈ {4, 5, 15}`. `sample` returns normally. MATLAB's window, `ii <= burn && ii > burn/2`, holds **two** iterations at `burn = 3`, so its estimate is not identically zero there — the odd-burn window difference and the zero floor interact, and at `burn = 3` MATLAB's chain keeps moving while gpyreg's freezes. Neither PyVBMC (`burn = thin*3 = 15`, or `thin*gp_s_N ≥ 5`) nor gpyreg's own default (`burn = thin*n_samples = 50`) reaches `burn ∈ {2, 3}`, and `fit` never passes a burn-in that small. I also confirmed by reading that gpyreg's window holds exactly `floor(burn/2)` iterations for both parities, matching its own `burn_stored` — so internal F11's neighbouring claim in "What I checked and found correct" is right, and the mismatch is on MATLAB's side, as comparison F1 says.

**On internal F9 (the `(-inf, -inf)` bound pair).** The pair is **shared**, not Python-only: `gplite_train.m:142` is `UB = max(LB,UB)`, and `gplite_covfun.m:131` and `gplite_noisefun.m:104` write `log(height*10)` and `log(height)` with `height = max(y) - min(y)`, so a constant target produces `-Inf` upper bounds in MATLAB exactly as it does in gpyreg (`covariance_functions.py:404`, `noise_functions.py:131`, `gaussian_process.py:428`). What differs is only what the optimizer does with the pair, and I did not determine `fmincon`'s behavior. The actionable part internal F9 identifies — that neither `get_recommended_bounds` nor `fit` rejects or repairs a pair no optimizer accepts — is Python's to decide, and the same repair would be MATLAB-side material.
