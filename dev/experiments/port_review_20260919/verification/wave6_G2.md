### The reach of the pooled bound recommendations (part (c) of the shared row), settled by reading and by `G2_4_reach_bounds.py`

### MATLAB's semantics at the edges (part (a) of the shared row)

`max`, `min`, `std` and `median` of a MATLAB matrix act along the first non-singleton dimension, so for a `1×D` row they act **along the row** (documented semantics; inferred, not run). `gplite` guards `y` against a single target (`if numel(y) <= 1; y = [0;1]; end`, at `gplite_covfun.m:106`, `gplite_meanfun.m:152`, `gplite_noisefun.m:96`, all transcribed in gpyreg) but guards `X` nowhere, and `gplite_train.m:74-76` calls the three `info` branches on whatever `X` it is given, so `N = 1` is reachable by a direct `gplite_train` call (not from `vbmc.m`, whose initial design has at least `FunEvalStart` points). With `N = 1` the two sides **swap places** (`G2_3_bounds.py` section F):

- `D = 1`: everything agrees. `width = 0` on both sides, so the four covariance bounds are `-inf` on both; `x0` is `-inf` on both, MATLAB through `log(std(scalar)) = log(0)` and Python through `np.std(X, ddof=1) = NaN` and the NaN-midpoint fill `0.5*(-inf + -inf)`.
- `D = 3`, `X = [[0.7, 2.0, -5.0]]`: MATLAB's `width = max(row) − min(row) = 7.0`, a scalar range **across dimensions**, so its covariance bounds are finite (`LB = [-11.8696]*3`, `UB = [4.2485]*3`) where Python's per-column widths are `[0,0,0]` and its bounds are `-inf`. The length-scale `x0` agrees (`1.3146` on both: MATLAB's `std(row)` with `D−1` normalization equals `np.std(X, ddof=1)` over the same `D` entries). For the **negative-quadratic mean the roles reverse and the two agree exactly** on `LB`, `UB`, `PLB` and `PUB` — Python's pooled reductions coincide with MATLAB's row reductions — differing only in `x0[0]` by the quantile convention.

So at `N = 1` MATLAB's mean-function bounds are built from a quantity that is not a per-dimension width either, which is a latent MATLAB defect in its own right (table row 4 below), and the pooled Python code happens to match it.

Proposed replacement of that entry, narrowed to what is true:

> ### NumPy's quantile convention in the GP mean-function bound recommendations
> - Python: `gpyreg/mean_functions.py:498-500` (comment: "For future reference note that quantile behaviour in MATLAB and NumPy is slightly different"), `:500-501` and `:508`.
> - MATLAB: `gplite/gplite_meanfun.m:157-160` and `:215-217`, via `gplite/private/quantile1.m`.
> - What differs: `np.quantile` interpolates between order statistics at `(i-1)/(N-1)`, while `quantile1.m` uses the `(i-0.5)/N` convention, so the plausible bounds of `mean_const` and the recommended starting value of the constant of a negative-quadratic mean differ by an amount that depends on `N` and on the spread of `y`: +0.046 and −0.119 on a 30-point set whose targets span 24 nats. > - Kind: deliberate change.

**(b) New entry for row G2-8:**

> ### `GP.quad`'s integral variance is normalized by the total training noise
> - Python: `gpyreg/gaussian_process.py:2188-2196`, used at `:2234`.
> - MATLAB: `gplite/gplite_quad.m:66-67`, `:101`.
> - What differs: MATLAB normalizes the Cholesky solves of the integral variance by `exp(2*hyp(Ncov+1)) * sn2_mult`, the constant noise hyperparameter, while `gplite/private/gplite_core.m:82` scales the factor by `min(sn2) * sn2_mult`, the minimum of the total training noise. The two coincide only when the constant term is the whole noise, so with user-provided or output-dependent noise MATLAB's `J_kk` is over-corrected and `max(eps, J_kk)` clamps the reported variance to machine epsilon. gpyreg uses the scale the factor carries, which is the right one: on a 14-point one-dimensional GP with one point at a tenfold noise variance, gpyreg and an independent grid quadrature of the latent predictive covariance agree to twelve digits (9.437668150943e-03) where MATLAB's formula gives 2.2e-16. The integral's mean agrees on both sides in every configuration.
> - Why: gpyreg 1.2.1, `acerbilab/gpyreg#49` ("`quad` scaled the Cholesky solves by the constant-noise hyperparameter, while the factorization is scaled by the minimum total training noise including `s2`"), issue #8's example (2.2e-16 against 0.2007), and the 1.2.1 release notes. `test_quadrature_with_noise_matches_numerical_integration` is the gate. No PyVBMC code calls `GP.quad`.
> - Kind: deliberate change (a fix of a MATLAB-side defect; `matlab_side_defects.md`).

**(c) New entry for row G2-9:**

> ### `predict(return_lpd=True)` pools the hyperparameter samples where `gplite_pred.m` does not
> - Python: `gpyreg/gaussian_process.py:2046-2064`.
> - MATLAB: `gplite/gplite_pred.m:124-127` with `:154-165`.
> - What differs: MATLAB computes the log predictive density per hyperparameter sample and returns the whole `(Nstar, Ns)` matrix even when the moments are averaged, leaving the pooling to the caller. gpyreg with `separate_samples=False` returns one column, the log density of the moment-matched Gaussian `N(mean_s mu_s, mean_s y_s2_s + var_s mu_s)` — neither the mean of MATLAB's columns (0.093 nats away on a three-sample 20-point fit) nor the log of the mean of the densities (0.181 nats). ### Anything I found beyond the two reports

- **Row G2-11 (mine).** `__core_computation`'s non-Cholesky branch computes `pL = solve_triangular(-L, …)` before the `if L is None: raise LinAlgError("Singular matrix for L Cholesky decomposition")` guard, so when all ten noise-inflation retries fail the caller gets `TypeError: bad operand type for unary -: 'NoneType'` instead of the intended message. Reproduced; the Cholesky branch in the same situation raises the intended `LinAlgError`.
- **Row G2-1's covariance is not positive semi-definite even after symmetrization** — the symmetrized matrix has eigenvalues −0.032 and −0.047 on my examples. Neither report measured that; both stopped at asymmetry. It matters for the disposition, because "symmetrize and carry on" is not a workaround.
- **The path frequencies of `__robust_cholesky`** (item 11 above) are mine: `direct` 96 / `eig p==0` 87 / `eig p>0` 21 over 144 swept cells, with the eigendecomposition confined to `D = 1` and the zero-factor path to `D = 1` with `Ntrain ≥ 40` and `sn = 1e-3`. They settle the disagreement the orchestrator named.
- **`sp.linalg.eig` does return complex eigenvectors for a rank-deficient symmetric matrix**, which `np.real` then truncates silently: sweeping 168 rank-deficient symmetric matrices of sizes 4 to 40 I found imaginary parts up to **0.572** (at `n = 6`, rank 2). The internal report's parenthetical (it observed up to 0.24) is therefore right, and it is a second, independent reason to use `eigh`: MATLAB's `eig` on a matrix it detects as symmetric uses the symmetric solver and returns real vectors (**inferred** from MATLAB's documented dispatch). Because G2-3 already breaks the factorization, this is currently masked; a fix of G2-3 that keeps `sp.linalg.eig` would still be wrong.
- **gpyreg's own `test_rank_one_update_with_heteroskedastic_noise` already constructs the state row G2-2 needs**, and its central assertion is the invariant `quad` violates. That makes the gate for G2-2 a one-line addition to an existing test rather than a new fixture.
- **`test_matern_isotropic_against_anisotropic` tolerates the degree-1 NaN explicitly** (`equal_nan=True`), so row G2-10 is not merely untested but is pinned as acceptable by a passing test. Any fix must change that assertion.
- **The `quad` averaging block's `ddof=1` is pinned by `test_quadrature_with_noise_matches_numerical_integration`**, which asserts `np.var(F_ref, ddof=1)` — so a change of that convention, were anyone to propose one, has a gate.
